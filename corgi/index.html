Foundational knowledge

What is an ETF?

An ETF is a regulated fund that holds a portfolio (stocks, bonds, commodities, crypto, etc.) and issues shares listed on exchanges. Corgi is the issuer; investors hold fund shares, not direct ownership of every underlying stock.

Two markets — primary vs secondary

ETF activity splits into two markets. Secondary is investors buying and selling existing ETF shares on exchanges — this is the vast majority of volume. Primary is Authorized Participants (APs) creating or redeeming shares directly with the fund — this changes shares outstanding and AUM, but happens far less often.

Primary market — creates, redeems & flows

The primary market is how ETF share count actually changes. Retail investors never touch it — only Authorized Participants can create new shares (deliver a basket of securities + cash) or redeem them (receive the basket back). Corgi does not profit on the spread; APs earn arbitrage when ETF price drifts from NAV.

Primary market data comes from the transfer agent + fund accounting flow ledger — not from exchange market data.

Secondary market — where ETFs trade

All exchange activity is secondary market. When the Daily Volume Report shows turnover on LSE, Xetra, or Euronext, that is investors trading existing shares — the fund's portfolio does not change. Europe is fragmented across venues; no single exchange dominates.

Data sourcing

Fund accounting requires five consolidated input datasets. Vendor redundancy (2–3 sources per market-data pipeline) provides failover and cross-check — not because two vendor feeds alone are sufficient. Holdings quantity, cash balances, shares outstanding, and trade history come from operational feeds; trial balance and NAV lines are computed from these inputs.

Vendor · Operational · Computed · Manual — GL sub-categories used throughout: BASE (cash/settlement), ETF (primary market flows), EQ (equity), FI (fixed income), ST (short-term).

Procurement paths

Direct enterprise feeds — required to own the system of record. Bloomberg Data License, LSEG/Refinitiv, and ICE are custom-quoted; typical range ~$50K–$150K/year at ETF-issuer scale for pricing + corp actions + reference. Redundancy adds cost for failover, not 3× data volume.
Administrator pass-through — published per-security rates when market data is sourced through the fund administrator. Source: U.S. Bank fund servicing fee schedule (SEC filing, 2025). Thematic equity fund (~100 holdings): ~$5K–$6K/year market data. Fixed-income fund (~200 bonds): ~$28K–$40K/year — FI pricing dominates.

Detail by datapoint

BASE — Cash

Settled and unsettled cash balances across all currencies, converted to fund base currency for the trial balance.

Source: Dataset 4 (custodian cash file). Inputs: fund ID, currency, settled balance, unsettled balance, as-of date.

BASE — Investment receivable / payable

Amounts due from sales not yet settled (receivable) or owed on purchases not yet settled (payable). These bridge trade date and settlement date.

Source: Computed from Dataset 4 trade history. Rule: For each open trade, principal ± fees where settle date > NAV date.

ETF — Accounts receivable (subscriptions) / payable (redemptions)

Cash owed to the fund from AP share creations not yet settled, or cash the fund owes on pending redemptions.

Source: Dataset 4 (TA confirms + AP flow ledger). Inputs: order type, shares, NAV used, settle date, cash component.

ETF — Subscriptions / redemptions

Capital flow from settled primary market activity. Net creates increase shares outstanding and AUM; net redeems decrease both.

Source: Dataset 4. Rule: Record at settlement — shares × NAV per share ± cash in lieu ± fees.

EQ — Cost of investments

Original purchase price of equity holdings, maintained per tax lot or aggregate, adjusted for stock splits, spin-offs, and mergers.

Source: Computed. Inputs: Dataset 4 trade history + Dataset 2 corp actions. Rule: Σ(buy qty × price + fees) − Σ(sell cost allocated) ± CA adjustments.

EQ — Net unrealized appreciation/depreciation

Difference between current fair value of equity holdings and their cost basis. Positive = unrealized gain; negative = unrealized loss.

Source: Computed. Formula: holding market value − cost basis, where market value = qty × mark price × FX (Dataset 1 + Dataset 4).

EQ — Dividends receivable & dividend income

Receivable: dividend declared but not yet paid. Income: recognized when the fund is entitled to the dividend (typically ex-date).

Source: Computed. Inputs: Dataset 2 (ex-date, gross rate) × Dataset 4 (shares held). Withholding applied per Dataset 3 tax rates.

ST / FI — Interest receivable & interest income

Coupon accrued on bonds and short-term instruments from last payment date to NAV date.

Source: Computed. Inputs: Dataset 3 (coupon, day-count, par) + Dataset 4 (face value held). Formula: f(coupon rate, day-count convention, par, days accrued).

FI — Amortization & accretion

Premium bonds: cost reduced over life (amortization expense). Discount bonds: cost increased over life (accretion income). Both use effective interest method.

Source: Computed. Inputs: Dataset 3 (issue date, maturity, coupon, OID/premium) + Dataset 1 (evaluated price at purchase) + Dataset 4 (qty).

ST — Realized gain/loss

Profit or loss recognized when a security is sold. Short-term if holding period < 1 year.

Source: Computed. Rule: sale proceeds − allocated cost basis (lot matching policy).

BASE — Accrued unitary fee expense

Daily accrual of the fund's unitary management fee. Reduces NAV each day; settles periodically per advisory agreement.

Source: Computed. Formula: AUM × TER (bps) × (days ÷ 365). Inputs: shares outstanding × NAV/share, fee schedule from fund master.

BASE — Dividend withholding tax payable

Tax withheld on foreign dividends, owed to the relevant tax authority. Paired with withholding tax expense on the P&L.

Source: Computed. Inputs: dividend income × withholding rate from Dataset 3 / tax policy.

BASE — Settlement breakage income

Small gain when actual settlement cash differs from expected (e.g. FX rounding, fractional shares). Common on ETF basket settlements.

Source: Computed. Inputs: expected settlement (trade terms) vs actual (custodian cash movement).

NAV — Net assets & NAV per share

Net assets = total assets − total liabilities. NAV per share = net assets ÷ shares outstanding. Published EOD to transfer agent, listing venues, and APs.

Source: Computed (net assets); Dataset 4 (shares outstanding). Formula: (Σ assets − Σ liabilities) ÷ shares.

NAV — Net income & total capital

Net income = total income − total expense for the period. Total capital = subscriptions/redemptions + accumulated unrealized G/L + realized G/L + retained earnings. Net assets must equal total capital + net income (trial balance check).

Source: Computed — rollup of all P&L and capital lines above.
