Foundational knowledge

What is an ETF?

An ETF is a regulated fund that holds a portfolio (stocks, bonds, commodities, crypto, etc.) and issues shares listed on exchanges. Corgi is the issuer; investors hold fund shares, not direct ownership of every underlying stock.

Two markets — primary vs secondary

ETF activity splits into two markets. Secondary is investors buying and selling existing ETF shares on exchanges — this is the vast majority of volume. Primary is Authorized Participants (APs) creating or redeeming shares directly with the fund — this changes shares outstanding and AUM, but happens far less often.

Primary market — creates, redeems & flows

The primary market is how ETF share count actually changes. Retail investors never touch it — only Authorized Participants can create new shares (deliver a basket of securities + cash) or redeem them (receive the basket back). Corgi does not profit on the spread; APs earn arbitrage when ETF price drifts from NAV.

Primary market data comes from the transfer agent + fund accounting flow ledger — not from exchange market data.

Secondary market — where ETFs trade

All exchange activity is secondary market. When your daily report shows turnover on LSE, Xetra, or Euronext, that is investors trading existing shares — the fund's portfolio does not change. Europe is fragmented across venues; no single exchange dominates.

What is a fund accounting system?

The system of record for the fund's official books: what it holds, what it's worth, how many shares exist, what flowed in/out, and what it owes. Not a reporting tool — reports are outputs. People and batch jobs change state inside it daily.

NAV & market value

Fund accounting answers one question every day: what is the fund worth, per share? That answer is the NAV (net asset value). Getting there means marking every holding to fair value, subtracting what the fund owes, and dividing by shares outstanding. The formulas are simple — getting the inputs right is the hard part.

Three numbers — don't mix them up

TermWhat it isWho produces itWhere it shows up
Holding market valueFair value of one position in the portfolioFund accountant — qty × mark price × FXInternal books; rolls up into NAV
NAV / shareOfficial fair value of one ETF shareFund accountant EOD batch (USBank → FG → in-house)Published to TA, exchanges, APs; NAV columns in daily report
ETF market pricePrice the share last traded at on an exchangeInvestors — supply and demand on secondary marketNot in fund accounting; APs watch it vs NAV

"Market value" in fund accounting always means holding market value inside the portfolio — not the ETF's exchange price.

Step 1 — Mark each holding

MVsecurity = quantity × mark_price × FX_rate_to_base_currency

The fund accountant picks the mark price from a hierarchy — not a markup formula:

PrioritySourceWhen used
1Exchange official closeLiquid equities, standard bonds
2Consolidated vendor (Bloomberg, Refinitiv)When exchange close unavailable
3Model / fair valueIlliquid bonds, halted names, private assets
4Manual overrideAccountant-approved exception

Step 2 — Roll up to NAV

Total assets = Σ(holding MV) + cash + receivables
Total liabilities = accrued management fee + custody + payables + …
NAV/share = (Total assets − Total liabilities) ÷ Shares outstanding

The management fee accrues daily as a liability and reduces NAV. That fee — a percentage of AUM — is Corgi's primary revenue from the fund. It is not a markup on exchange price.

Step 3 — Publish and reconcile

Official NAV goes out EOD to the transfer agent, listing venues, and APs. On the secondary market, the ETF can trade slightly above or below NAV (premium / discount). APs arbitrage that gap through the primary market — creating when price > NAV, redeeming when price < NAV. That keeps exchange price close to NAV; the arb profit goes to APs, not the issuer.

What actually breaks NAV

The arithmetic almost never fails. Wrong NAV comes from bad or stale inputs:

InputSourceTypical failure
QuantityCustodian holdings file, rec'd internallyBreak vs internal books — wrong qty
Mark pricePricing vendorStale, missing, wrong security, halted
FXFX vendor EODWrong date, wrong currency pair
Shares outstandingTransfer agentCreate/redeem not yet reflected
Corp actionsOps / vendor alertsSplit, merger, dividend not applied
CutoffBatch scheduleLate file → wrong day's prices in NAV
